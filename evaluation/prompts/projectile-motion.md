# Benchmark Prompt: Projectile Motion

## Educational Goal
Create an interactive learning experience that teaches the physics of 2D projectile motion under gravity.

The learner should develop an intuitive understanding of the independence of horizontal and vertical velocity components, how launch angle and initial velocity determine peak height and horizontal range, and why $45^\circ$ maximizes range in a vacuum.

## Learner Experience & Requirements
1. **Initial Condition Controls**:
   * The learner should be able to adjust key physical parameters such as launch angle ($0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$), initial launch velocity ($v_0$), and gravitational acceleration ($g$).
2. **Interactive Simulation**:
   * The learner should be able to launch the projectile, step frame-by-frame through time, pause mid-flight, adjust playback speed, and reset to the launch pad.
3. **Visual Representation**:
   * Animate the projectile moving across a coordinate plane along its parabolic trajectory.
   * Trace the flight path as it evolves over time.
   * Display real-time physical telemetry (e.g. current elapsed time $t$, horizontal position $x$, vertical altitude $y$, instantaneous vertical velocity $v_y$, and total flight distance).
4. **Trajectory & Telemetry Analysis**:
   * Visualize the trajectory curve and time-series changes in velocity or altitude.
   * Clearly indicate flight milestones such as launch, peak altitude (where vertical velocity $v_y = 0$), and ground impact.
5. **Prediction & Active Inquiry**:
   * Prompt the learner to hypothesize how changing launch angle or doubling initial velocity affects maximum range before running the flight.
6. **Pedagogical Explanation**:
   * Explain the governing kinematic equations for horizontal and vertical displacement.
   * Explain why horizontal velocity remains constant in ideal projectile motion while vertical velocity changes at a constant acceleration.
7. **Knowledge Check**:
   * Include a short assessment with immediate feedback testing conceptual understanding of trajectory mechanics and optimal launch angles.
